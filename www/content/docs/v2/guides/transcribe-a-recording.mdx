<GuideVideo slug="transcribe-a-recording" poster="/guides/transcribe-a-recording/poster.webp" title="Downloading a speech model, transcribing a recording, and citing it" />

A recording becomes a source through a speech recognition model on your computer: its transcript is searched and cited like any document, and each passage says when it was said. Your audio never leaves your computer. See [Recordings](/docs/v2/sources#recordings).

This guide uses a LibriVox reading of *The Art of War*, which is in the public domain.

<Steps>
<Step>

### Choose a speech recognition model

Open **Settings**, then **Speech recognition**, and click **Add model**. Each model lists its languages; one detects the language on its own. Click **Download**: downloading a model also chooses it.

![The speech recognition models on offer, with Download](/guides/transcribe-a-recording/01-speech-models.webp)

</Step>
<Step>

### Add the recording

Add a `.wav`, `.mp3`, `.flac` or Ogg Vorbis file to **Sources** like any other file. Its row shows how far the transcript has got.

![The recording in Sources, being transcribed](/guides/transcribe-a-recording/02-transcribing.webp)

</Step>
<Step>

### Ask about it

When it's ready, ask about it in a chat. A cited passage starts with the time it was said, such as `[00:02:41]`.

![A cited passage from the recording, with the time it was said](/guides/transcribe-a-recording/03-cited-recording.webp)

</Step>
</Steps>

Under the model in use, **Spoken language** sets the language your recordings are in. A recording can't be played back in SurfSense yet.
