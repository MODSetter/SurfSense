# ADR 0059: A born-digital PDF is read from its own text layer, and Docling reads only a PDF with a page that needs it

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-16
- **Source:** measured on a Ryzen 7 5800X under Windows, with the embedder at FP32 ([ADR 0058](0058-the-bundled-embedder-runs-at-fp32-on-x86-64.md)), recorded in [documents](../architecture/documents.md#pdfs-with-a-text-layer)

## Context

Every PDF went to Docling with OCR and table structure on: about 3.0 s and 32 CPU-s a page, up to 5.5 s on table pages, plus 11 to 17 s to import Docling and load its models in a worker's first job. A 46-page budget took two minutes to be searchable, and the one ingest thread ([ADR 0008](0008-two-job-queues.md)) held every upload behind it. A born-digital PDF already holds its text, and pypdfium2, which the worker ships, reads it in milliseconds a page. On born-digital budget pages Docling's OCR changed nothing: with it off, the Markdown was byte-identical.

Docling's Markdown was not the better text for this app either. In an answer-quality A/B, Basic mode, on 50 questions over five born-digital City of Boston PDFs (four budget volumes and a standard contract) and a Word contract, with four models and retrieval through the app's own chunker and hybrid search, answers from the text layer scored 140/200 = 0.70 strict and from Docling's Markdown 124/200 = 0.62. The gap was in table cells, 48/92 against 35/92; prose and contract clauses tied. Docling makes every bold label a `##` heading and the chunker splits at headings first, so a table's passage was cut off from the program or department it belongs to, and its padded pipe tables split one table into three or more passages that named nothing. The answer was in the top 5 passages for 41 questions against 38.

What the text layer cannot see: a page that is a picture, such as a scan or a cover photo; a scanner's invisible OCR layer, which may be wrong; garbage from a broken font map; a turned page, whose lines pdfium returns last to first; and a filled form's answers, which live in its fields, not on the page.

## Decision

- A `.pdf` is read from its text layer when every page passes a triage that takes milliseconds: a page of running text (`ok:text`), a page with under 20 characters and almost no image (`ok:sparse`), an empty page (`ok:blank`), or at least 20 characters over images covering most of the page, as on a section cover (`mixed:big-image`). At least one page must be `ok:text`. The rules are in [documents](../architecture/documents.md#pdfs-with-a-text-layer), and the code in [`pdf_text_layer/`](../../surfsense_local/backend/worker/ingestion/pdf_text_layer/).
- Any other page, a turned page, a filled form field, an empty text layer or a pdfium error sends the whole file to Docling, which reads it as before and keeps its figures. The choice is made for the whole document, for safety in a release: no source's text is spliced from two readers, and a PDF that needs Docling anywhere reads exactly as it did before. Splicing Docling's pages in by page range can follow once it is measured. Amended: a PDF with some failed pages, at most half of them, now has only those pages read by Docling, by page range, and the rest from its text layer, while a scan, a filled form, a file pdfium cannot read and one with no `ok:text` page still go to Docling whole ([documents](../architecture/documents.md#pdfs-with-a-text-layer)).
- The text is the A/B's text arm: each page's text as pdfium lays it out, and pages a blank line apart. Three changes: a word hyphenated at a line's end is joined again, control codes are dropped, and a blank line is added where a paragraph ends, found from the gaps between its lines, so the chunker cuts there and not at an arbitrary line. Only `\n` is added; the A/B's characters are kept as they were. It has no Markdown headings and no pipe tables.
- Ingest keeps no figures for a text-read PDF. By the maintainer's decision they come from the figures-only pass when the agent or Studio first asks for them. That pass reads born-digital pages with Docling's layout model alone, about 6 CPU-s a page against 32 for a full conversion, and gives way to waiting sources between page runs. For a PDF of at most 40 pages whose pages hold image objects, ingest also queues the pass at the lowest priority, so it runs once nothing else waits.
- `SURFSENSE_LOCAL_PDF_TEXT_LAYER=off` sends every PDF to Docling, as before. A value it cannot read also means off.
- **Rejected: a Docling pass afterwards** over pages that have a text layer, to replace their text with Docling's. Docling's text answered worse, and the pass would double the CPU and change chunk ids under existing citations.
- **Rejected: LiteParse** as the reader. Its tables were the best of the fast tools, but it gave empty Markdown on a turned page and 76 false code blocks in 30 pages, dropped a column, bundles a second PDFium, and downloads Tesseract data by default.
- **Rejected: TableFormer's FAST mode** to speed Docling up. It got 26 of 53 rows right on a 53-row table.
- **Rejected: Docling's classic PDF backend.** It was 1.3 to 1.55 times as fast, but lost the heading "Program 2. Valuation" from a budget.

## Consequences

- Measured to `ready` through the upload route and the pipeline, Docling warm: a 12-page executive summary 16.7 s to 1.4 s, a 46-page operating budget 125.6 s to 9.0 s, and a 33-page LaTeX spec 73.1 s to 3.8 s, 12 to 19 times as fast; CPU fell from 176, 1,327 and 863 CPU-s to 10, 63 and 29. Reading and triage take about 7 ms a page; chunking and embedding take the rest. Reading such a PDF never imports Docling, so its ingest also skips the cold start and about 1 GB of memory; only a figures-only pass loads Docling, after the file is ready.
- 32 of the 36 PDFs measured are read from their text layer. Of the 19 Boston budget PDFs, two go to Docling for a cover photo whose title is under 20 characters, and one for a raster org chart whose page holds only its running header. A filled form goes to Docling too.
- Through the shipped code, on the A/B's questions with Gemma 3 4B, Qwen3.5-9B and Haiku 4.5, the text layer scored 105/150 = 0.70 strict against Docling's 90/150 = 0.60, and 35/69 table cells against 24/69. The second corpus was six PDFs: a two-column paper, a deck exported to PDF, the spec and three reports. There it scored 52/69 against 54/69: better on charts (6/9 against 2/9), level on slides, and worse on tables (19/30 against 27/30). Most of that loss is one paper's Table 1, whose caption and header the chunker cut from its rows; the rest is a table whose rows begin with a number.
- Content that exists only as pixels on a page with running text is not in the text. That covers a raster chart or org chart under 65% of the page, and a picture under a cover's title. Docling's Markdown had lost these as well. A wrong ToUnicode map gives fluent but wrong letters, which are indexed as they are; no reader tested caught it.
- Docling reads only the runs of pages that need it, when they are at most half the file; a scan, a filled form and a file pdfium cannot read are still read whole, so a scan gains only the embedder's speed-up.
- A text-read PDF's figures arrive after it is ready. Until the pass has run, the agent hears that its images are being extracted, and Studio's first draft places none from it.

## Where the code stands

- Measured on one machine, and the stress PDFs are generated. The table loss on the second corpus was in chunking and line geometry, not the triage. Paragraph breaks keep Table 1's caption, header and rows in one passage, and put the answer in the top 5 passages for 22 of 23 questions there (20 before) and 44 of 50 on Boston (41). Measured again, answers scored 0.83 to Docling's 0.78 on the second corpus, table cells 28 of 30 to 27, and 0.70 to 0.60 on Boston (Gemma 3 4B and Qwen3.5 9B), where Gemma lost three table cells to passages that now pack across a page break. Rows that begin with a number are still open.
