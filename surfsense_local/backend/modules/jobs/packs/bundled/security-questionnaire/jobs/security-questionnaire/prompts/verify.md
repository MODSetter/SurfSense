Decide whether they are evidence for that answer:

- At least one quote must be about the very control, practice or fact the question asks about, for the same people or systems. A quote about a different control, a different system or a different subject is not evidence, even when it sounds related: a backup procedure is not an encryption policy, a rule for the firm's own staff accounts says nothing about the accounts of the product's customers, and keeping a record for itself is not a promise to send it to the client.
- Judge by what a passage is about, not the words it uses: a policy that covers the practice under another name counts, such as vendors for suppliers or team members for employees.
- A quote about all of the firm's policies, such as who approves them or how often they are reviewed, is evidence for those parts for each of the firm's own policies, standards and procedures, including "in-scope" or "controlled" documents, unless a quote leaves that document out. Beside a quote showing a written policy, standard, procedure or practice on the question's subject, under that name or another, it is evidence for those parts: no quote need say that this one document is reviewed. On its own it does not show that the firm has the policy or practice the question names.
- Each subject the question names counts: a question about A, B and C needs a quote about each of them. A quote about one, beside a statement about all policies, leaves the others open.
- Read the question's parts as a reviewer would: a policy on the subject that staff must follow shows it is established, documented, communicated and applied; a stated approval or periodic review shows it is approved, evaluated and maintained; any stated cycle meets "regularly"; one of the alternatives a question offers is enough; a rule for everyone covers each group within it; examples after "e.g.", "for example" or "such as" show a kind and need not each be named, while items the question says are included are parts; and nothing the question does not name is needed.
- For Yes, the quotes must say that the firm does it, or that a policy requires it. For No, that the firm does not do it, or that a policy forbids it. A quote that only fails to say Yes is not a No, and neither is a rule whose stated scope leaves part of the question out. For N/A, that the firm does not have, use or offer the thing asked about. A quote that says a provider runs it for the firm, or that it falls outside a programme's or a policy's stated scope, is not evidence for N/A.
- They need not repeat every detail the question lists; other passages may cover the rest. But a number, time limit, frequency or kind the question names is not a detail: "at least annually", "within 24 hours" or a "tamper-evident" seal needs a quote that states one that meets it.
