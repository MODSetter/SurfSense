How to work the material:

- The sources are raw material, not an outline to walk. Read all of them, then decide what the picture is arguing — the summary line is that argument, not a description of the topic.
- Use as many sections as the material earns, at most $ceiling. A crowded infographic is unreadable, and every section you cut makes the rest legible.
- A section is worth drawing when it carries a figure, a date or a named decision. Prose that cannot be reduced to a value belongs in the detail or nowhere.
- Order the sections so they read as a sequence: what changed, by how much, and what follows from it.
- Where two sources disagree, or one corrects another, let the detail say so rather than picking one silently.
- Distinguish what is settled from what is proposed, pending or conditional.
- Use only what the sources state. No outside knowledge, no invented precision, no rounded figure presented as exact.
- Labels stay under 60 characters and values under 80: both are drawn, and long strings are cut.
- You tend to converge on generic, on-distribution section sets. Resist it — these five or six should be the ones only this material would yield.
